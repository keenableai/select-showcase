From a two-power duopoly to a multipolar sky: 7,370 orbital launch attempts from 65 sites

Asked:

“Every orbital launch from 1957 to today — for each one list the year, the launch site, the operating bloc and whether the site is still active.”

Jonathan McDowell’s GCAT (snapshot 2026-08-31) records 7,370 orbital launches and confirmed failed attempts from 1957 through 2026, flown from 65 launch sites by 20 operating blocs. Of those sites, 41 still have an open-ended pad on the books, 4 are recorded closed, and 20 have no clear status in the pad registry.

The Soviet Union and Russia together account for 3,345 launches, edging past the United States’ 2,454 — yet Baikonur’s historic complexes sit in the “status unclear” band of the pad registry — planet4589.org
Eight blocs debuted after 1990 — Russia, Brazil, North Korea, a Cayman-flagged operator, Iran, South Korea, New Zealand, Germany and Australia (the last two in 2025) — planet4589.org
Sites whose every recorded pad is closed hosted only 54 of the 7,370 launches: nearly all launch history flowed through infrastructure that is still open-ended or unresolved in the registry — planet4589.org

Operating bloc is GCAT’s LVState mapped to readable names; unmapped codes kept as-is. “Active” means at least one registered pad at the site has an open-ended stop date (TStop='*') — an infrastructure statement about the site, not proof the precise historical pad in a row still operates; “Inactive” means every recorded pad has a stop date; otherwise status is unclear. Mobile and air launches keep their GCAT site designation; site_code is the stable identifier and terse GCAT short names are not expanded.
