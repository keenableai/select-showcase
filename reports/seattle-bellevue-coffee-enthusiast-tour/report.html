Eight cafés, two days: taste Seattle's espresso lineage on Wednesday, then Bellevue's global brews the morning after

Asked:
“Plan a coffee tour through seattle / Bellevue for a coffee enthusiast”

An 8-stop tasting route built from researched café pages: day 1 threads five Seattle shops from Pioneer Square through downtown to Capitol Hill; day 2 takes three Bellevue stops from Old Bellevue's Main Street into downtown Bellevue. Each day runs roughly 8:00 to early afternoon — a tasting progression, not a caffeine maximum.

The route at a glance

Hours and public tastings change. Click each stop's source and recheck shortly before visiting — especially Victrola's cupping, and the Elm and Ghost Note listings.

What the timeline can't say

Do Seattle on a Wednesday: Victrola's roastery lists a public cupping Wednesdays at 11am — confirm directly with Victrola before building the day around it. victrolacoffee.com
Start early at Elm: its listed hours end at 1pm Monday and 3pm Tuesday–Friday, and it shows closed on weekends — and its source is a blog post, so verify with extra care. elmcoffeeroasters.com
Ghost Note's hours come from a third-party directory, not an official page — treat them as a rough guide and double-check the short Tuesday (7am–1pm). restaurantji.com
Keep the days separate: Seattle and Bellevue are designed as two distinct crawls — don't cross Lake Washington mid-tour. Bellevue opens gently at Bellden, daily 8am–3pm. belldencafe.com

How to order at each stop

  • Elm — espresso-and-filter comparison; ask about the One & One or One of Everything flight.
  • Olympia — a seasonal single-origin as filter or espresso; compare extraction with Elm.
  • Victrola — join the listed 11am cupping; taste, don't gulp.
  • Ghost Note — a creative seasonal signature; share one drink.
  • Espresso Vivace — straight espresso or a small milk drink for texture and traditional craft.
  • Bellden — the pour-over program; optionally share the Rosemary Burnt Honey Latte.
  • Café Hagen — Scandinavian-influenced coffee, roasted by Hagen, with a pastry or brunch item.
  • Third Culture — finish globally: Indian filter, Vietnamese, or New Orleans coffee.

Caffeine management checklist

  • Order the smallest serving offered; split drinks with a companion.
  • Drink a glass of water at every stop.
  • Eat — a pastry at Hagen, brunch where offered; never taste on an empty stomach.
  • At cuppings, sip and spit like the pros; you're there for flavour, not dose.
  • Leave 60–90 minutes between stops and walk the legs — the route is built for it.
  • Stop when palate or pulse says so; the cafés will still be there tomorrow.
