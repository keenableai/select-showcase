Migration strategy follows geography: ocean loops, wetland chains and land-bridge corridors carry 12 famous species around the world

Asked:

“How do birds migrate in different parts of the world? List most famous species and their routes, draw them on a map”

Twelve representative long-distance migrants, drawn from 53 cited route waypoints across government, scientific and conservation sources (assembled 31 Aug 2026). Routes span the Arctic Tern’s ≈71,000 km pole-to-pole year to the Bar-tailed Godwit’s ≈11,000–12,000 km nonstop trans-Pacific leg. Distances are annual, one-way or single-leg as noted.

How migration works, region by region

Atlantic and Pacific ocean loopsSeabirds ride prevailing winds in immense figure-eight and looping circuits: the Arctic Tern from Greenland to the Weddell Sea, the Sooty and Short-tailed Shearwaters around the whole Pacific rim.
Americas: coastlines and the GulfThe Red Knot chains hemisphere-spanning coastal stopovers such as Delaware Bay; the Ruby-throated Hummingbird and Blackpoll Warbler cross the Gulf of Mexico and open Atlantic in single flights.
Europe–Africa soaring corridorsWhite Storks depend on thermals over land, so they funnel through narrow land bridges and skirt the Mediterranean rather than crossing broad water.
Central and East Asian wetland chainsSiberian Cranes step from Yakutia through the Songnen Plain and Bohai coast to Poyang Lake — each wetland an indispensable link.
East Asian–Australasian tidal-flat networkBar-tailed Godwits and Great Knots stage on Yellow Sea mudflats (Yalu Jiang, Bohai) between Siberia or Alaska and Australasia; the godwit’s return is a nonstop ocean crossing.
Trans-Indian-Ocean falcon flightAmur Falcons gather in Nagaland, India, then cross the open Arabian Sea to winter in southern Africa — a rare over-water passage for a raptor.

Findings

The Arctic Tern’s tracked Greenland population averages ≈71,000 km a year between the Arctic and the Weddell Sea — the longest known animal migration. natur.gl
The Bar-tailed Godwit’s fall return from Alaska to New Zealand is a single ≈11,000–12,000 km nonstop flight over the open Pacific. usgs.gov
The Alaskan Northern Wheatear, a songbird of ~25 g, travels ≈14,600 km one way across all of Eurasia to winter in East Africa. royalsocietypublishing.org
East Asian routes cluster on the Yellow Sea: godwit, Great Knot and Siberian Crane all depend on the same Chinese coastal wetlands. birdlife.org
