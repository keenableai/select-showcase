Search engines number in the hundreds — this index catalogues 1,253 names, and 2023–24 alone launched more than any earlier decade’s AI wave

Asked:

“List all search engines existed in history. Make sure not to miss any modern ones.”

This is a living research index, not a complete census — no authoritative registry of search engines exists, and “all” cannot be proven. It covers 1,253 catalogued public-facing search-service names from timelines, lists and 2023–2026 AI-search sources (current to 31 Aug 2026): 390 have a sourced launch year (1982–2026), 100 are explicitly ended or inactive, 258 are classed as AI search or answer engines, and 863 names have no resolved launch year — blank means unresolved, never a guess.

What the picture cannot say alone

The 2020s already hold 125 dated launches — 113 of them AI search or answer engines, versus 12 in the whole 1990s. piccard.it
The 2000s were the deadliest dated era: 30 of its 106 launches are explicitly marked ended or inactive. blog.mkohli.com
863 of 1,253 names — over two thirds — carry no sourced launch year at all, mostly regional and short-lived services that were never well archived. zonalatina.com
The earliest name in the catalogue predates the web: WHOis, from 1982, eight years before Archie crawled FTP listings in 1990. en.wikipedia.org

Method: 1,253 normalized public-facing search-service names gathered from historical timelines, engine lists, and modern regional, privacy, decentralized and 2023–2026 AI-search sources, current to 2026-08-31. Included: WWW search engines, metasearch, search portals, notable vertical/specialized engines, decentralized/privacy engines, and named AI answer/search products. Excluded: site-internal search boxes, browser find, desktop-only file search, enterprise-only software, and API-only products. Launch/end years and statuses appear only when sourced; blanks are unresolved, not inferred. Counts and era totals come from the precomputed summary rows. Category boundaries are fuzzy and false positives may remain: treat this as a living research index, not an exhaustive registry. Long category strings are trimmed in the table for space.
