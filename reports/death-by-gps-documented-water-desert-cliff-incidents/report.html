Drivers trusted the turn-by-turn voice into 59 waters, 25 deserts and 19 cliff edges

Asked:
“Map every documented case of a driver following GPS into a lake, desert, or cliff — 'death by GPS' incidents”

A live-web scan on 31 August 2026 surfaced 103 distinct reported incidents after semantic filtering and incident-level deduplication, spanning 2003–2026 across at least 20 countries. “Death by GPS” is the established umbrella term: the census includes fatal events, survivals, rescues and near-disasters. This is a documented-web census, not an exhaustive global registry.

What the record says, beyond the map

Water dominates: 59 of 103 incidents (57%) end in a lake, river, harbour or canal — from a BMW driven down a ferry ramp into the Havel river in Germany in 2005 tapesearch.com to a car in a Thai irrigation canal in 2025 theglobeandmail.com.
The deadliest single case in the set: a mother followed her GPS onto a gravel road in Death Valley in August 2009; her son died before rescue — the most heavily corroborated incident here, with 48 independent pages thetimes.com.
Utah's Grand Staircase–Escalante is a repeat trap: the same monument roads appear in incidents from 2003, 2007, 2008 and 2017, stranding everyone from couples to a 26-person convoy automotive-fleet.com.
The first legal precedent: in 2009 a UK driver whose satnav left his BMW teetering over a 100-ft drop in Todmorden became the first person convicted for blindly following satnav directions dailymail.com.
Evidence is thin for much of the tail: 45 of 103 incidents rest on a single accessible page, while the average incident is corroborated by 5 pages — the ledger below shows each count as a confidence cue.

News coverage is uneven; some dates are month/year or relative wording and are left unresolved; precise coordinates were not published, so map positions are approximate; some incidents rest on one accessible source. “Death by GPS” is an umbrella term — the set includes rescues, survivals and near-misses as well as 23 incidents whose outcome text reports at least one death.
