Thirty-eight Big Things still stand, and one straight-line loop can touch every state and territory

Built from 940 attraction-level rows extracted in a live web scan on 27 August 2026, deduplicated to 38 named, standing Big Things across all 8 Australian states and territories, plus 3 that have closed. No source page stated exact coordinates, so every marker sits at its locality’s approximate position, joined by a nearest-neighbour straight-line route.

The tradition began in the 1960s with the Big Scotsman (1962, Medindie), the Big Banana (1964, Coffs Harbour) and the Big Murray Cod (1968, Tocumwal) — all three still stand on this map. theconversation.com
Not every Big Thing survives: the Big Wool Bales in Hamilton (closed 2020), the Giant Gippsland Earth Worm in Bass (closed 2020) and the Big Cask Wine in Mourquong (closed 2012) are excluded from the route and listed separately below. theconversation.com
The category is still growing: the Big Watermelon in Chinchilla went up in 2018, and Carnamah’s Big Tractor opened in 2024 after organisers raised more than $600,000. theconversation.com
Big Things now appear on Australia Post coins covering every state and territory, including the Big Jumping Crocodile in Wak Wak, the Big Lobster in Kingston and the Giant Ram in Wagin. southernhighlandnews.com.au
