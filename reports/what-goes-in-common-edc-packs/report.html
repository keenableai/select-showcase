Starter packs distilled from the diagram

Universal core

  • Flashlight, wallet, pen, keys, phone
  • Pocket knife or multi-tool
  • Lighter, small first-aid kit, bandage
  • Power bank, water bottle, notebook
  • Sunglasses, lip balm; tourniquet and paracord for the safety-minded

Commuter add-ons

  • Laptop or tablet, mouse, charger and cables
  • Headphones or earbuds
  • Umbrella, book, lunch and snack
  • Hand sanitizer, jacket

Prepared add-ons

  • Headlamp, compass, map
  • Water filter or purification tablets, extra water and food
  • Duct tape, tarp, ferro rod, emergency blanket, poncho
  • Whistle, fire starter, bandana, sunscreen, hat, gauze

Context matters: pocket knives, fixed blades, pepper spray and fire starters are regulated differently by city, workplace, transit system and airline — check local rules before carrying. Medical gear like tourniquets is only as useful as the training behind it. EDC is preparedness for daily life, not a weapons kit.

Method: broad web research on 2026-08-31 across EDC specialist sites, forums, blogs, packing lists, commuter/work-bag and outdoor/preparedness sources. Raw extraction yielded 4,670 pocket-item rows, 4,307 commuter rows and 12,339 prepared rows before aggregation; the 60-item shortlist keeps items appearing in multiple archetypes or clearing archetype support thresholds. A count is the number of distinct pages in the searched corpus mentioning an item within an archetype after synonym consolidation (phone variants, knife variants, multi-tool spellings, notebook/notepad, power-bank, headphone/earbud and first-aid variants) — not an ownership rate. Sources mix recommendations with actual loadouts, so read this as a synthesis of common guidance, not a population survey. Region totals come from the precomputed aggregate set.
